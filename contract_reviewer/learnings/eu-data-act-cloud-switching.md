# EU Data Act: cloud switching and unfair B2B data terms

Compiled 2026-10-11. Source: Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act), EUR-Lex text, Articles 13, 23-31 and 50 (restated, not copied).

**Consult when:** a cloud, SaaS, PaaS or IaaS contract (a "data processing service") is sold to a customer in the EU, especially its termination notice, exit / migration fees, egress charges, or data-retrieval window; or a B2B contract sets terms on data access and use.

**Application dates (Art. 50).** Applies from **12 September 2025**. Chapter IV unfair-terms rules apply to contracts concluded after 12 September 2025, and from 12 September 2027 to earlier contracts of indefinite duration or expiring at least 10 years from 11 January 2024.

**Switching rights the contract must give the customer (Art. 25).** These are mandatory; contrary terms do not bind the customer.
- Maximum **notice period to start switching: 2 months** (Art. 25(2)(d)).
- Switching completed within a mandatory maximum **transitional period of 30 calendar days** after the notice period (Art. 25(2)(a)); if technically unfeasible, the provider must say so within **14 working days** and may propose up to **7 months** (Art. 25(4)); the customer may extend the transitional period once (Art. 25(5)).
- Data **retrieval period of at least 30 calendar days** after the transitional period (Art. 25(2)(g)).
- Exhaustive specification of exportable data and assets, and data erasure after the retrieval period.

**Switching charges (Art. 29).**
- **From 12 January 2027: no switching charges at all**, including data-egress charges for switching (Art. 29(1)).
- 11 January 2024 to 12 January 2027: only **reduced** switching charges, **not exceeding the provider's costs directly linked to the switch** (Art. 29(2)-(3)). A flat exit or "migration assistance" fee above cost is not allowed now and no fee is allowed from 2027.
- Not covered: services whose main features are mostly custom-built for one customer, and time-limited non-production test versions (Art. 31).

**Unfair terms (Art. 13).** A term on data access/use unilaterally imposed by one business on another does not bind if it is unfair: it grossly deviates from good commercial practice in data access and use, contrary to good faith. Terms reflecting mandatory EU law are not unfair. The article lists terms always unfair (e.g. excluding liability for intentional acts or gross negligence, excluding remedies for non-performance) and presumed unfair.

**Review rule.** For an EU customer, flag: termination notice over 2 months, retrieval window under 30 days, any switching / exit / egress fee above direct cost (and any at all for switches after 12 January 2027), and no data-export specification. Contracts signed before 12 September 2025 are still covered by the switching rules from that date.
