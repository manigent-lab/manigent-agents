# §4 Non-competes: state and federal status

Compiled 2026-10-10 (as-of dates inline). Section numbers §1-§8 refer to the topic files listed in INDEX.md.

**Consult when:** any agreement restrains post-relationship work or competition, or makes a worker repay money or forfeit pay on leaving.

**Say it plainly.** When a statute below voids the clause on the facts given, the answer is "void / unenforceable under [section]", unhedged, and the worker can take the job as far as that covenant is concerned. One sentence of caveat at most, and only if a fact could change the result (e.g. a sale-of-business exception, a different signing date, a trade-secret claim that survives separately). The state-by-state table with numbers and sources is `knowledge/noncompete-enforceability-2026.tsv`.

**California.**
- **[§16600](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-16600/)** voids restraints on a lawful profession, trade or business except the statutory exceptions (e.g. sale of business, dissolution). Subdivision (b) codifies *Edwards* (2008): employment non-competes are void "no matter how narrowly tailored". Subdivision (c) reaches restraints even where the restrained person is not a party.
- **[§16600.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-16600-1/):**
  - It is unlawful to include a non-compete in an employment contract, or to require one.
  - Employers had to send individualized written notice by **Feb 14, 2024** to current employees, and to former employees employed after **Jan 1, 2022**, that such clauses are void. Notice went to the last-known address and email.
  - A violation is unfair competition under §17200.
- **[§16600.5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-16600-5/)** (SB 699, effective **Jan 1, 2024**):
  - A void contract is unenforceable "regardless of where and when the contract was signed".
  - An employer may not attempt enforcement even if the contract was signed, and the work done, outside California.
  - An employer may not enter into such a contract.
  - Doing so is a **civil violation**. Employees, former employees and prospective employees may sue for an injunction and/or actual damages, plus **attorney's fees and costs** if they prevail.

- **Stay-or-pay ([§16608](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-16608/), [Labor Code §926](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-926/), AB 692 of 2025).** For contracts entered on or after **Jan 1, 2026**, it is unlawful to include or require a term making a worker repay a debt, or pay a penalty, fee or cost (training repayment, quit fees, replacement-hire costs), when employment ends; the term is void. Worker remedy: actual damages or **$5,000 per worker**, whichever is greater, plus injunction and attorney's fees. Exceptions: government loan programs; tuition for a *transferable credential* (separate agreement, not a condition of employment, amount fixed in advance at no more than cost, prorated, no repayment if fired other than for misconduct); approved apprenticeships; housing leases/loans; and a discretionary sign-on payment only if it is in a **separate agreement**, the worker had **5 business days** and notice of the right to consult a lawyer, the retention period is **at most 2 years**, repayment is **prorated and interest-free**, the worker could defer the payment to the end of the period, and repayment is owed only on a voluntary quit or a misconduct firing. Mid-employment retention clawbacks are not covered by the exception.

**Minnesota [§181.988](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.988)** (2023 c. 53 art. 6 s. 1; applies to agreements entered on or after **July 1, 2023**, per [Stinson](https://Stinson.com/newsroom-publications-minnesota-non-compete-ban-signed-into-law-and-effective-july-1-2023)):
- Non-competes are void.
- "Employee" **includes independent contractors**: anyone not paid on a W-2, including an entity the individual was required to form.
- Excluded from the ban: NDAs, trade-secret protections and non-solicits.
- Exceptions: sale of business, and dissolution.
- Courts may award attorney fees.
- Subd. 3: a worker who primarily lives and works in Minnesota cannot be required to litigate or arbitrate elsewhere or under other law. Such a clause is voidable by the worker.

**FTC rule: gone.**
- *Ryan, LLC v. FTC*, 746 F. Supp. 3d 369 (N.D. Tex. 2024) set the rule aside nationwide.
- On **Sept 5, 2025** the FTC voted 3-1 to dismiss its 5th and 11th Circuit appeals and accede to the vacatur ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule)).
- 16 CFR part 910 was removed effective **Feb 12, 2026** ([91 FR 6507](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-12/html/2026-02866.htm)).
- The FTC still pursues non-competes case by case under Section 5.

**Other states.**
- **North Dakota [§9-08-06](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t09c08.pdf):** restraints are void except for sellers of goodwill and partners/members/shareholders on dissolution, within a reasonable area and time.
- **Oklahoma 15 O.S. §219A:**
  - A former employee may compete, but may be barred only from *directly soliciting* established customers.
  - Conflicting terms are void ([Gable, Feb 2026](https://www.gablelaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-02-26-Employment-Alert-Restrictive-Covenants-for-Oklahoma-Employees.pdf)).
  - The same alert reports a federal court enforcing a mandatory out-of-state forum clause over Oklahoma policy (Oct 2025). Forum clauses can decide which state's policy is ever applied.
- **Wyoming [W.S. 1-23-108](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2025/Enroll/SF0107.pdf)** (SF0107, 2025): any covenant not to compete that restricts a person's right to be paid for skilled or unskilled labor is **void** for contracts entered on or after **July 1, 2025** (earlier contracts are untouched). Exceptions: sale of a business or its assets; protection of trade secrets (only to that extent); recovery of relocation, education and training costs on a sliding scale (up to 100% if the employee served under 2 years, 66% for 2 to under 3 years, 33% for 3 to under 4 years); and executive and management personnel, officers, and employees who are professional staff to them. Physician non-competes are void (the rest of the agreement survives).
- **Virginia [§40.1-28.7:8](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title40.1/chapter3/section40.1-28.7:8/):** no employer may enter into, enforce or threaten a non-compete with a "low-wage employee". Since **July 1, 2025** (SB 1218) that includes **every FLSA non-exempt (overtime-eligible) employee, whatever they earn**, as well as anyone earning below the Commonwealth's average weekly wage; commission-driven earners are excluded. From **July 1, 2026** (2026 c. 883, SB 170, new subsection C), **no** non-compete is enforceable against an employee **discharged without cause** unless the employer provides severance or other monetary payment that was **disclosed when the covenant was signed**. Civil penalty $10,000 per violation; courts may void the covenant and award lost pay, liquidated damages and fees.
- **Washington (RCW 49.62).** Until June 29, 2027: void unless annualized earnings exceed **$126,858.83** (employees) or **$317,147.09** (contractors) for 2026 ([WSR 25-20-099](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2025/20/25-20-099.htm)); over 18 months presumed unreasonable. **From June 30, 2027** ([ESHB 1155, ch. 149, Laws of 2026](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1155-S.SL.pdf), signed March 23, 2026): **all** non-competes are void **regardless of when signed**; it is a violation to enforce, threaten, claim one applies, or enter one; employers must make reasonable efforts to send written notice by **October 1, 2027** to current and former employees and contractors whose covenant is still running. "Non-compete" now includes no-accept / no-service-of-customers clauses and any clause forcing repayment or forfeiture because the person competes. Non-solicits survive only for customers the worker directly developed, **max 18 months**. Exceptions: sale of goodwill or of an ownership interest of **1% or more**, compliant franchise sales, NDAs, trade secrets, education-cost repayment expiring within 18 months of start and prorated. Remedy: actual damages or **$5,000**, whichever is greater, plus fees.
- **Texas health care ([Bus. & Com. Code §15.50](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB01318F.htm), SB 1318).** For covenants entered or renewed on or after **Sept 1, 2025**, a physician non-compete must: allow a **buyout of no more than the physician's total annual salary and wages** at termination; **end within 1 year** of termination; cover **no more than a 5-mile radius** from where the physician primarily practiced; be clearly and conspicuously in writing; and give patient-list access. It is **void** if the physician is involuntarily discharged without good cause. §15.501 extends the rules to dentists, licensed nurses and physician assistants. Purely administrative (managing medical services) roles are outside.
- **Florida CHOICE Act ([§§542.41-542.45](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0542/0542.html), 2025).** Pro-enforcement. For a "covered employee" (salary above **twice the annual mean wage** of the county of the employer's principal place of business; excludes health care practitioners), a non-compete of **up to 4 years** (and garden leave up to 4 years) is enforceable if the employee got it **at least 7 days** before the offer expired, was advised **in writing** of the right to counsel, and acknowledged in writing receiving confidential information or customer relationships. Courts **must** grant a preliminary injunction, dissolvable only on clear and convincing evidence. Applies to agreements on or after July 1, 2025 (secondary source). Everything else stays under §542.335, where an employee restraint over **2 years** is presumed unreasonable.
- **Tennessee (Public Chapter 934, HB 1034, effective July 1, 2026; secondary source only):** non-competes void for employees earning under **$70,000** a year; restrictive covenants of 2 years or less presumed reasonable for employees. Primary text not read; see gaps-and-sources.md.

**Decision rule.** Check the signing date against each effective date (Wyoming, Virginia, Texas, Florida and Tennessee apply to new agreements; California §16600.5 and Washington from 2027 reach old ones too). Check the worker's state, signing date vs effective dates, employee vs contractor (Minnesota covers both), law/forum clauses, and whether any sale-of-business exception is genuine (restricted person actually sells goodwill or equity).
