# §8 Review procedure and escalation triggers

Compiled 2026-10-10 (as-of dates inline). Section numbers §1-§8 refer to the topic files listed in INDEX.md.

**Consult when:** you start a full-contract review, or need to decide whether a lawyer must be involved. For a closed question (is this clause enforceable, what is the market position, list the issues) skip the procedure and answer directly.

1. **Frame the deal:** our side, contract type, value (annual fees × term), data sensitivity, jurisdictions of parties and workers.
2. **Inventory the stack.** List the master terms, cover page or order form, DPA, SLA and security exhibit, plus any URL-incorporated terms. Note the precedence clause and any unilateral-change rights.
3. **Read definitions first.** Flag each defined term that widens or narrows a right: CI, Customer Data, Feedback, Deliverables, Affiliates, Losses.
4. **Map each clause to §1–§7.** Classify it as **Standard / Acceptable fallback / Deviation / Walk-away**.
5. **Check symmetry.** Compare each obligation for both sides: caps, indemnities, assignment, termination, notice periods.
6. **Run the numbers:** cap dollars at signing and month 12, renewal notice deadlines, price-escalation ceiling, service-credit maximum.
7. **Statutory overlay.** Non-competes (§4), privacy terms (§5), auto-renewal (§6), and the DTSA notice for individuals.
8. **Redline with rationale.** One comment per change, citing the standard (e.g. "Bonterms §16.1: 12-month fees") and offering the fallback.
9. **Summarize.** Top issues by dollar exposure and likelihood, open items with owners, and risks accepted by the business.

**Escalate to a lawyer when** (one line in the answer, naming the point, only when it changes what the user does next):
- our side bears uncapped exposure, or the cap is below the plausible data or regulatory loss;
- a restrictive covenant's enforceability turns on facts you were not given or a state not covered in non-competes.md (a covenant a listed statute plainly voids needs no escalation: say it is void);
- cross-border transfers, or health, children's, financial or special-category data, are involved, or the counterparty refuses Art. 28 or §7051 terms;
- core platform IP ownership or copyleft code is at stake;
- foreign law, arbitration or an unfamiliar forum is involved, or the deal is in a regulated sector;
- whether a SaaS subscription is a "service" contract under NY §5-903 decides the outcome;
- the deal exceeds internal signing authority, or the counterparty is in litigation or insolvency;
- a clause cannot be classified, or documents in the stack conflict.
