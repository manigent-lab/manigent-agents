# HIPAA business associate agreements (BAAs)

Compiled 2026-10-11. Sources: 45 CFR 164.504(e), 164.314(a), 164.410 and 164.502(e) (eCFR / Cornell LII copies, public law, restated not copied).

**Consult when:** a vendor will create, receive, maintain or transmit protected health information (PHI) for a covered entity (provider, health plan, clearinghouse) or for another business associate, or a contract mentions PHI, patient data or a "business associate agreement".

**When a BAA is required.** Any vendor that handles PHI on the covered entity's behalf (hosting, billing, IT support with access to systems holding PHI, scheduling, analytics). Pure conduits and a treating provider receiving PHI for treatment are not business associates. A BA must in turn sign BAAs with its own subcontractors (164.502(e)(1)(ii)). No BAA in place before PHI flows = an impermissible disclosure by the covered entity. Make signing the BAA a condition precedent to access, not a promise to sign later.

**Required terms, 164.504(e)(2) (check each).** The BAA must:
- [ ] set the BA's permitted and required uses and disclosures (none the covered entity itself could not make), optionally allowing the BA's own management/administration and data aggregation;
- [ ] (ii)(A) bar any other use or disclosure;
- [ ] (ii)(B) require appropriate safeguards, and Security Rule compliance for electronic PHI;
- [ ] (ii)(C) require reporting of any use or disclosure not allowed by the contract, including breaches of unsecured PHI (164.410) and security incidents (164.314(a)(2)(i)(C));
- [ ] (ii)(D) require subcontractors that handle PHI to agree to the same restrictions (flow-down);
- [ ] (ii)(E)-(G) make PHI available for individuals' access, amendment and accounting of disclosures;
- [ ] (ii)(H) comply with the Privacy Rule when carrying out a covered entity's obligation;
- [ ] (ii)(I) make internal practices, books and records available to the HHS Secretary;
- [ ] (ii)(J) at termination, return or destroy all PHI if feasible, otherwise extend protections and limit further use;
- [ ] (iii) let the covered entity terminate if the BA violates a material term.

**Breach notice timing.** 164.410(b): the BA must notify the covered entity **without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days** after discovery (discovery = first day the breach is known, or would have been known with reasonable diligence). A BAA allowing more than 60 days is non-compliant. Covered entities usually negotiate a much shorter contractual deadline because their own 60-day clock to individuals can run from the BA's discovery when the BA is their agent; that is negotiation, not law.

**Not law yet.** The HIPAA Security Rule NPRM (published Jan 6, 2025) would require BAs to report contingency-plan activation within 24 hours and to verify safeguards annually. As of 2026-10 it is **not final** (agenda moved from final-rule stage; reported target July 2027). Do not tell a user it is required; offering it as a negotiated term is fine.

**Commercial points that are issues** (not required by HIPAA but material): a liability cap that swallows BAA breaches (put breach costs, notification and credit monitoring under a super-cap or uncapped); no indemnity for the BA's HIPAA violations; cyber insurance; offshore access to PHI; de-identification rights the BA reserves for itself (must meet 164.514).

**Not issues by default:** a BAA that lets the BA use PHI for its own proper management and administration (164.504(e)(4)); return-or-destroy with an "infeasible" carve-out that extends protections; reporting of unsuccessful security incidents (pings, port scans) by a standing notice in the BAA.
